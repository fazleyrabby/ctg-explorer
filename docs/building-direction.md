# Building direction for Little Chittagong

Reference: [Jalan KL](https://kl.jalanmalaysia.com/), inspected in its globe and close city views on 2026-09-28. Its appeal comes from legible landmark silhouettes, varied small buildings, contrasting roof colors, trees and street-level props arranged in compact blocks. Use those composition ideas, not its specific models or Kuala Lumpur landmarks.

## Build next, in order

1. **Old-town shop rows — Chawkbazar, Anderkilla and Khatunganj.** Replace some isolated apartment blocks with short attached runs of two- to four-storey shops. Vary widths, terracotta/teal roofs, deep ground-floor awnings, shutters, small signs and rooftop water tanks. Leave walkable gaps and courtyards between runs. This gives the most visible improvement at walking scale without enlarging the map.
2. **Port and riverfront warehouses — Chittagong Port and Khatunganj.** Use wide sawtooth or pitched roofs, loading doors, cargo canopies, painted bay numbers and stacked goods. Keep the port's cranes and container yard as the focal point; warehouses form a compact backdrop.
3. **Distinctive civic anchors — Court Building, New Market and railway station.** Give each a recognisable outline from above: the Court Building's long hilltop facade and prominent entrance, New Market's connected shopping blocks, and the station's long platform roofs and ticket hall. Give each a small planted forecourt or plaza so it reads as a destination.
4. **Agrabad commercial cluster.** Use a limited group of taller stepped office buildings with vertical fins, dark glass bands, balconies and roof equipment. Avoid repeating one tower shape at different heights. Keep this as the only clearly tall district so visitors can orient themselves.
5. **Neighborhood houses and roadside stalls.** Mix one- and two-storey houses with taller flats near the main road. Add roof terraces, stairs, laundry, small corner tea stalls and shaded benches sparingly. A few detailed corner plots do more than hundreds of identical boxes.

## Visual rules

- Use five or six coordinated wall colors: warm cream, dusty coral, muted yellow, sea teal, pale blue and lilac. Pair each with terracotta, teal or off-white roofs; use saturated color mainly on awnings, doors and signs.
- Give every building one strong silhouette feature: a pitched roof, parapet, balcony stack, arcade, corner tower or canopy. Avoid adding every detail to every building.
- Group small buildings into blocks facing the road. Put awnings, paths, planters, benches and trees in the spaces between them so the land feels occupied without filling every plot.
- Keep hero landmarks more detailed than background buildings. From overview, visitors should recognise each district by its skyline and roof pattern; at walking scale, doors and street-level details should carry the scene.
- Reuse geometry and materials across variants to retain the current draw-call budget. Keep road and flyover clearance and the compact playable bounds.

## First implementation pass

Start with the old-town shop rows and a small set of roadside stalls around Chawkbazar/Anderkilla. They will replace the most obvious repetitive mid-rise pattern in the current overview and offer the clearest Jalan KL-inspired improvement without changing the world layout.
